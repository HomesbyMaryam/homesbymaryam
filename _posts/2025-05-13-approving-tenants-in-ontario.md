## Overview

Tenant screening represents a vital responsibility for Ontario landlords and property managers. Selecting qualified tenants protects your investment, ensures consistent rent collection, and minimizes expenses related to evictions or property deterioration. Ontario's regulatory environment, however, imposes specific obligations on property owners regarding both tenant selection procedures and human rights protections.

## Understanding the Legal Framework

Ontario landlords must operate within two primary legislative structures:

- **Residential Tenancies Act (RTA)**
- **Ontario Human Rights Code (OHRC)**

Under the OHRC, it is illegal to discriminate against tenants based on protected grounds such as race, religion, age, marital status, family status, disability, sexual orientation, gender identity, and others. Landlords may implement objective, business-focused criteria provided they do not violate these protections.

## Key Steps in Tenant Screening and Approval

### 1. Create a Clear Rental Application Process

Develop a comprehensive, legally compliant rental application form requesting:

- Full name and contact information
- Employment details
- Income verification
- Previous landlord references
- Credit authorization consent

### 2. Verify Employment and Income

Confirm applicants possess stable and adequate income to cover rent costs, typically following the "3x rule" (income represents at least three times monthly rent). Acceptable documentation includes:

- Recent pay stubs
- Employment letter
- Bank statements
- Proof of savings

### 3. Conduct Credit and Background Checks

With written authorization, request credit reports from agencies like Equifax or TransUnion. Examine:

- Credit score and history
- Debt levels
- Payment patterns
- Public records (bankruptcy, collections)

### 4. Check Rental History and References

Contact previous landlords to verify:

- Rent payment history
- Lease violations
- Property condition upon move-out
- Evictions (if any)

> Always verify references are legitimate by cross-checking phone numbers and addresses.

### 5. Personal Interview (Optional)

Meeting applicants enables evaluation of communication, professionalism, and demeanour. However, avoid questions that could breach the OHRC, such as inquiries regarding family status, ethnic origin, or disabilities.

### 6. Apply Consistent, Written Criteria

Establish a documented tenant selection policy and apply it uniformly to prevent discrimination allegations. Acceptable criteria include:

- Ability to pay rent
- Creditworthiness
- Good rental history
- Clean background (if legally obtained)

## Important Considerations

### Protect Privacy

Handle all personal information according to Ontario's privacy legislation (Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act — PIPEDA).

### First-Come, First-Qualified

To minimize bias allegations, certain landlords adopt a "first-come, first-qualified" approach — granting the unit to the initial applicant meeting the established criteria.

### Avoid Pitfalls and Discrimination

Do not reject applicants solely because they are newcomers, receive social assistance, are students, or have children — these practices may violate the OHRC.

### When You Can Legally Refuse a Tenant

Legal grounds for rejecting applicants include:

- Inability to pay rent
- Bad credit history
- Poor landlord references
- Incomplete application
- Providing false information

## Conclusion

Tenant approval in Ontario necessitates equilibrium between rigorous screening and strict legal compliance. Through implementation of a transparent, equitable, and uniform process, landlords can safeguard their properties while respecting tenant protections established under the Ontario Human Rights Code and the Residential Tenancies Act.
