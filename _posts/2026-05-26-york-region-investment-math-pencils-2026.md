A semi on the King Road corridor that would have cleared roughly a thousand dollars a month in net rent in 2019 now produces a thinner margin, and in several listings I have run the numbers on this spring, none at all once a realistic capital reserve is included. The arithmetic of holding rental property in York Region has shifted under investors' feet over the last three years, with mortgage rates, property tax assessments, and insurance premiums all moving in the same direction. What still works is a narrower set of property types than it was, but the deals are there for buyers who read the pro forma carefully and walk away from the ones that do not.

## What the rent ceiling actually looks like

Rents in York Region have softened from their 2023 peak. Per CMHC's January 2026 Rental Market Update, the average two-bedroom purpose-built rental in the GTA settled at $2,228 in late 2025, with York Region tracking a touch above that at roughly $2,310. On the secondary market (the slice most small investors compete in), Rentals.ca's April 2026 report put the York Region two-bedroom average at $2,750, down 4.1 percent year over year.

Single-family rentals tell a slightly different story. King City detached homes are leasing between $3,800 and $5,200 depending on lot, age, and finish. Aurora's Bayview Northeast and Bayview Hill in Richmond Hill have seen four-bedroom executives hold near $4,200 to $4,800. Kleinburg estate rentals can clear $6,000, but the pool of qualified tenants at that price point is thin, and vacancy windows often stretch past forty-five days.

The takeaway is that the top of the rent band has effectively stopped advancing, while the next chapter of the story (carrying costs) has not.

## Carrying costs that have outgrown the rent curve

Five-year fixed mortgage rates for investment properties are pricing in the 5.10 to 5.45 percent range per the Bank of Canada's April 2026 monetary policy report and current lender postings, with insured options not available on rentals over the $1M threshold. Variable rates sit slightly higher after the Bank held its policy rate at 2.75 percent through the spring.

Property tax in King Township is currently 0.937 percent of assessed value for the 2026 levy (per the Township of King's 2026 budget approval). Aurora's combined rate is 0.901 percent; Richmond Hill sits at 0.832 percent. On a $1.7M property, that is roughly $14,100 to $15,900 annually before utilities or insurance.

Landlord insurance in York Region has moved meaningfully. Brokers are quoting rental policies on detached homes between $2,400 and $3,600 a year, roughly double the 2020 figure, driven by both rebuild cost inflation and a harder claims environment for water and weather events.

## Where the math still works in York Region

Three property profiles continue to produce defensible returns in the current cycle:

A King City detached with a legal accessory unit. Under the Township's 2023 zoning amendments allowing accessory dwelling units on most residential lots, a basement suite or coach house can lift gross rent by 30 to 45 percent on the same envelope of carrying costs. The CMHC MLI Select program (still active per CMHC's January 2026 program notes) offers preferred financing terms when energy efficiency, affordability, or accessibility commitments are met.

A Kleinburg or Hills of St. Andrew property with a legal lower suite already in place. Verified rental income, separate utilities, and a registered second unit shorten underwriting and protect insurance pricing. Premiums for properties with proper egress and ESA-certified electrical run noticeably lower than for unregistered suites.

A Bayview Hill or Unionville townhouse priced under $1.4M with strong school-catchment demand. The rent-to-price ratio is leaner than King City detached, but vacancy is the lowest in the region, and tenant turnover costs are correspondingly modest.

## The numbers behind a representative pro forma

Consider a $1.85M King City property with a legal lower unit, purchased with 25 percent down. The mortgage of $1,387,500 at 5.20 percent on a 30-year amortization produces a monthly payment of approximately $7,580.

Property tax at 0.937 percent: $1,445 monthly.
Insurance: $250 monthly.
Utilities (landlord portion, common areas, water): $180 monthly.
Maintenance and capital reserve (1.0 percent of value annually): $1,540 monthly.
Vacancy allowance (3 percent): $216 monthly.

Total monthly carrying cost: roughly $11,211.

Combined rents (main: $3,900; lower: $2,400): $6,300 monthly.

That is a monthly shortfall of $4,911 before the principal portion of the mortgage is credited back as equity build (about $1,820 in year one). Net cash drag: $3,091 monthly, or $37,092 annually. Against the $462,500 down payment and closing, the property needs roughly 4.0 to 4.5 percent annual appreciation just to break even before tax, before any equity build.

That math is not promotional. It is the reason underwriting discipline matters now more than at any point in the last decade. Investors comfortable with the appreciation thesis on King City land (a thesis I think holds, given the King Road corridor's planning context and the constrained detached supply across York Region) can absorb it. Investors counting on cash flow alone cannot.

## What to verify before you sign

Confirm the zoning permission for any secondary suite in writing through the municipality, not the listing. Township of King, Aurora, and Richmond Hill all maintain accessory dwelling unit registries, and policies have changed twice in three years.

Ask for the previous twelve months of rent rolls, not market projections. A vendor's stated rent and a tenant's actual paid rent are not always the same number.

Request the insurance loss run on the property. Two prior water claims will reprice your policy materially.

Build a true capital reserve into the pro forma. A roof, a furnace, two windows, and a driveway are not optional line items on a thirty-year hold. Underwriting that assumes otherwise is wishful, not conservative.

Where the deal works after all of that, it tends to work durably. Where it does not, walking away in May is far less expensive than discovering it in November.

The investors writing offers across York Region this spring are doing so with a sharpened pencil, not a wider net. Their patience is showing up in the kind of properties that close: the ones where the math was already close to working before the negotiation began.
